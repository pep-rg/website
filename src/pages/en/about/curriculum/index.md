PEP consists of two strands: **Projects (P)** and **Skill Workshops (SW)**.

## Projects (P)

Students conduct their own research projects on issues they are strongly interested in. Through these projects, they explore an inquiry of their own and present the results in English. First- and second-year students choose topics from their everyday lives, such as club activities or university classes. Third-year students conduct academic projects on topics closely related to their fields of study. Fourth-year students write an English abstract of their graduation thesis, written in Japanese, and give a presentation on their research.

Project classes are not streamed by proficiency, because students with lower English proficiency do not necessarily lack presentation or communication skills. Students who are less confident in English take an active part in class by drawing on their own strengths and expertise.

### P1

Students conduct a project on a topic from their everyday lives and aim to give an attractive self-presentation. They learn to gather information about their surroundings and describe it in English, building English skills for situations such as job interviews.

### P2

Students read English-language papers on their topic, write summaries, and conduct research using questionnaires or interviews. They then present their results and answer questions from their peers, developing the presentation skills needed to introduce a workplace or laboratory, explain products, and receive visitors in English.

### P3

Students conduct research in groups of four or five and hold debates and panel discussions in English, learning and using the expressions of academic English. They gain the ability to carry out group projects, presentations, discussions, and negotiations in English.

### P4

Students learn methods of academic writing and apply them to their projects, summarizing the results in a term paper of around 2,000 words and presenting it. They acquire the English skills needed to write and present academic papers.

## Skill Workshops (SW)

Skill Workshops aim to improve the four integrated skills of listening, speaking, reading, and writing. As in Projects, students are strongly encouraged to communicate in English with their teachers and with each other. Classes are streamed by proficiency, and students develop the four skills through interactive tasks that make the most of the grammar, vocabulary, and expressions they learned in junior and senior high school. These skills are then put to use in Projects for exchanging information, expressing opinions, giving presentations, and writing academic papers.

### SW1 (Listening)

Students get used to the sounds of English discourse. Through interactive tasks that integrate speaking, reading, and writing, the course strengthens listening ability.

### SW2 (Speaking)

Students improve their speaking through interactive tasks called the “Leadership Method,” discussing information gathered from listening and reading and summarizing it in writing.

### SW3 (Reading)

Students read at least two English articles every week, including homework. The tasks also involve speaking, listening, and writing about what they have read.

### SW4 (Writing)

Students write extensively both in and out of class and improve by revising texts corrected by their teachers. Because they write about what they have heard and read and then talk about what they have written, they build a foundation in all four skills.
