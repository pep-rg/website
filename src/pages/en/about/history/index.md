## Origins at Keio University SFC

The Project-based English Program (PEP) was first designed and put into practice by Dr. Yuji Suzuki at Keio University Shonan Fujisawa Campus (SFC), which opened in 1990.

SFC encouraged all classes to be run as projects, making use of the state-of-the-art ICT of the time. Dr. Suzuki adopted a project-based approach not only in his courses for first- and second-year students, such as Project English and Language and Communication, but also in specialized subjects including Modular English, English Diversity, English in Social Context, Language Communication Theory, and Context and Semantics.

Students focused their research on their own interests and communicated their findings to a global audience — in English in English courses, and in Japanese in other courses. Details can be found in Dr. Suzuki’s book *Eigo Kyoiku no Grand Design: Keio Gijuku Daigaku SFC no Jissen to Tenbo* (The Grand Design of English Education: Practice and Prospects at Keio University SFC; Keio University Press, 2003).

## Chiba University of Commerce and Hokuriku University

In 2000, at the request of the late Hiroshi Kato, then president of Chiba University of Commerce (a founder of SFC and the first dean of its Faculty of Policy Management), and under Toshiaki Izeki, the first dean of the university’s newly established Faculty of Policy Informatics, Dr. Suzuki introduced the program as an English advisor and achieved good results.

In 2004, at the request of Haruichi Sonoyama, dean of the Faculty of Future Learning at Hokuriku University, Dr. Suzuki introduced the program as an education advisor. Classes were taught by Associate Professor Masaru Yasuda and by Tsukasa Yamanaka as a part-time lecturer. Beyond the English courses, Sonoyama and Yamanaka also co-taught a project-based course titled “World Languages and People.”

## Introduction at Ritsumeikan University

In 2007, Dr. Suzuki was visited at SFC by Yoshihiro Taniguchi and other members of the committee establishing Ritsumeikan University’s College of Life Sciences and College of Pharmaceutical Sciences (Taniguchi later became the first dean of these colleges), which opened in 2008. After hearing about the program, they decided to introduce it at the new colleges.

From November 2007, as a visiting professor at Ritsumeikan University, Dr. Suzuki refined the latest version of the program. After taking early retirement from SFC in March 2008, he joined the College of Life Sciences and the College of Pharmaceutical Sciences in April and implemented the program. In November 2007, at the request of Professor Kei Ameyama of the Graduate School of Science and Engineering, the program was also run for a short period in the graduate school’s GP program for developing internationally minded engineering graduate students, which helped identify issues and improve the program.

The program was introduced to the College of Sport and Health Science when it opened in 2010, to the College of Comprehensive Psychology when it opened in 2016, and to the College of Arts and Design when it opened in 2026.

At SFC, the program was centered on Dr. Suzuki’s own classes and formed only a small part of the overall English curriculum; it was not offered to all students (about 450 per year). Reflecting on this, Ritsumeikan made the program available to every student in the new colleges (about 420 per year). Close communication among English faculty, faculty across the colleges, college executives, and administrative staff — including specialist English projects co-taught by subject-area and English faculty — has earned high praise from inside and outside the university. As a result, Projects and Skill Workshops interact organically, leading to high-quality project outcomes and rising scores on tests such as the TOEIC.

## How the program is run

Projects at all levels are taught mainly by full-time faculty and full-time contract English instructors, who meet weekly to exchange detailed feedback on their classes, placing great emphasis on communication and collaboration. The teaching materials and methods were developed by Dr. Suzuki and the teachers in the program.

Skill Workshops consist of intensive, interactive drills designed to build the English proficiency students need for Projects. They are delivered in partnership with a specialist external education provider, with whom the English faculty have jointly developed materials and methods.

For students who lack the basic English skills the program requires, we offer Brush Up English, an online program to raise their English level before enrollment (for internal and recommended admissions) and after.

## Research is education, education is research

Research on the theories of language and communication that underpin the program is also actively pursued. This research has been conducted on the basis of the overall design of the program Dr. Suzuki established at SFC, and its results are applied to and reflected in practice. At the same time, the program’s classroom practice informs further research. In other words, the philosophy that “research is education, and education is research” runs through PEP.
