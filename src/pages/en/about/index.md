The Project-based English Program (PEP) is a required English curriculum for undergraduates in the College of Life Sciences, the College of Pharmaceutical Sciences, the College of Sport and Health Science, the College of Comprehensive Psychology, and the College of Arts and Design at Ritsumeikan University. The program aims to develop the strong, productive English skills that students need in their fields.

The curriculum consists of two strands: **Projects**, in which students present the results of their own research in English, and **Skill Workshops**, which strengthen the four skills of English. To evaluate changes in English ability we use the TOEFL and TOEIC tests, and we are also developing our own assessment model for the program.

## Preparing students to lead at the forefront of globalizing academic fields

The life sciences and many other academic fields are now deeply engaged in international collaborative research, and even at conferences in Japan it is common for presentations to be given in English. In response to this globalization of academic disciplines, the colleges that run PEP offer it as a common required curriculum to train the productive English skills essential for success in these fields.

PEP aims to train students to gather information from around the world, discuss it, and communicate the results globally in English — in other words, to build the foundations of global communication. However good the content is, if it cannot be expressed convincingly in English, students cannot fully take part in their fields. Productive English skills are indispensable in the global era.

In PEP lessons, students pursue issues they are personally interested in and discuss them in English. The program is designed so that students progressively acquire the ability to participate in global activities using English.

## The two pillars: Projects and Skill Workshops

In **Projects**, students research their own themes and communicate them in English. In **Skill Workshops**, they improve their basic English skills in proficiency-streamed classes. Projects make students realize the need to improve their productive ability; Skill Workshops reinforce the basic skills, which are then put to use again in Projects. This interplay produces a strong synergistic effect.

Because researchers today often present their work in video form, the program actively uses ICT (Information and Communication Technology). By creating spaces where students can pursue their chosen themes in depth, we encourage autonomous learning.

- **Projects**
  - Students choose their own research themes, explore them through research, and share their ideas.
  - First- and second-year students give presentations, debates, and panel discussions on familiar themes such as daily life and classes. In the second semester of the second year, students write a term paper of about 2,000 words.
  - Third-year students work on themes from their specialist fields in English and give poster presentations (Life Sciences and Pharmaceutical Sciences).
  - Fourth-year students (optional) write an English abstract of their graduation thesis and give an oral presentation (Life Sciences and Pharmaceutical Sciences).
- **Skill Workshops**
  - Classes dedicated to the four foundational skills of listening, speaking, reading, and writing.
  - Interactive classes focusing on the four skills each semester, aligned with Projects, allow students to polish their basic and practical abilities effectively.
  - First-year students are placed based on the TOEFL-ITP; second-year students are streamed based on results such as the TOEIC test taken in December of their first year.

## Results through close collaboration with the colleges

There are two reasons why students’ four skills improve in this program. First, a curriculum that integrates the exploration and communication of students’ own interests generates genuine motivation. Second, because the program is taken by all undergraduates and the college executive takes final responsibility for English education, not only the English faculty but the whole college works together to promote it.

Because PEP is required for all undergraduates, even students who feel negatively about English are motivated by their peers. By collaborating closely with faculty in specialist fields, we also aim for English education that connects smoothly to students’ academic disciplines. Through PEP, we will continue to improve the program so that students build a solid foundation in English and the ability to keep refining their skills on their own after graduation.
